Today I want to share a couple of links related to American agriculture. Ok, this is not my area of expertise, but these two women have been doing a lot to open my eyes to understanding what's happening in rural America, with our food, with our farmers and farm laborers, and how we talk about it.

The first is a newsletter/blog from Sarah Mock, author of _Farm (And other F Words)_. She writes about farming and agriculture, but for n00bs like me who have driven through miles of wheat without really understanding what we're seeing. Not all of her posts are 'on topic', but she periodically posts things like this that really fill in some blanks for me. Here's her latest post:

https://sarahmock.substack.com/p/the-farmers-share-of-the-food-dollar

She also released a podcast (mini-series?) called 'The Only Thing that Lasts' where she talked through the history of agriculture in America, from the founding to the modern day.

https://ambrook.com/offrange/podcast/the-only-thing-that-lasts

The second person I want to point you to is Sarah Taber. Her background is in food science and safety, and she is a small farmer in the southeastern US. I started reading her newsletter when she was running for office, and it seems like now she's mostly working on YouTube videos (well, and her real job). I find her blunt style very refreshing. She's spent some of the last summer talking about food safety in the framing of our latest lettuce debacle, but if you go back further there are videos on biological sexism, the fertilizer 'crisis', Ballerina Farm, raw milk, and regular bouts of of ag-related politics.

https://www.youtube.com/@FarmToTaber
